Of all the problems an owner can have, sewage coming up through the drains ranks among the most dangerous. Anyone who comes in contact with contaminated material is exposed to parasites, bacteria and viruses, among them giardia, E. coli, salmonella and hepatitis, all carried in raw sewage and all dangerous. When it happens in Williamsburg, Williamsburg Restoration is available 24/7 with technicians trained for this work, proper protective gear and disinfectants registered with the EPA, everything needed for a safe decontamination.

Sewage damage is classified as Category 3 (black water), the most contaminated and dangerous category of water damage. That rating calls for the strictest protocols: containment barriers go up, crews wear full protective equipment, every porous material the sewage touched is removed, and the surfaces that stay are disinfected thoroughly. Our technicians follow IICRC S500 standards for Category 3 water damage so your property is restored to safe occupancy standards.

Here is what the work covers: standing sewage is pumped out, contaminated water is extracted from the structure, porous materials the sewage touched (drywall, insulation, carpet and padding) are torn out and disposed of properly, hard surfaces are disinfected thoroughly, antimicrobials are applied, and the structure is dried so mold does not follow. We also deodorize the affected area with industrial-grade hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging equipment to eliminate lingering sewage odors.

How to Tell You Need Sewage Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Lower levels or drain openings giving off a heavy sewage odor, sometimes like rotten eggs
  • Dark, dirty water or sewage rising out of a tub, toilet or floor drain
  • Sluggish drainage at all fixtures, a sign the main line is blocking up
  • Dark residue from sewage left on the basement floor or ringing a drain
  • Damp patches on a wall or floor that also smell foul
  • Several fixtures backing up at once instead of only one
  • Toilets or drains that gurgle whenever you run water somewhere else

Maintenance Tips for Sewage Cleanup

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • In an emergency a plumber needs the cleanout, so learn where yours is and keep it in good shape
  • Keep downspouts and sump pump discharge out of the sanitary sewer so it does not overload in heavy rain
  • Never pour grease, oil or food waste down your drains, since these cause blockages
  • A drain that is slowing down should be dealt with early, while it is still partly open
  • Install a backwater valve on your sewer connection to prevent backups from the municipal system
  • Have roots removed where trees grow by the sewer, before they get inside the pipe and plug it

Why Professional Sewage Cleanup Matters

No homeowner should try to clean up sewage personally. Handling raw sewage safely takes specialized training, EPA-registered disinfectants that consumers cannot buy, and personal protective equipment, meaning respirators, Tyvek suits and protective boots and gloves. Hepatitis, gastrointestinal disease and infections of the skin or respiratory system are among the serious illnesses these pathogens cause.

Contaminated materials count as biohazard waste, and disposing of them properly is part of a professional cleanup. It is illegal to throw sewage-contaminated insulation, drywall or carpet in the regular trash, and doing so puts the community and the workers who collect it at risk. Licensed biohazard disposal facilities take this waste from certified restoration firms, which keep the required protocols in place.
