Why Burst Pipe Repair Is Not a DIY Job

The damage from a burst pipe tends to reach well past the wet areas in plain view. Materials get saturated in spots that can stay undiscovered for days or weeks, because the water runs along conduits, joists, floor assemblies and the insides of walls. By then, mold may already be established in hidden spaces and structural materials may be significantly weakened. Professional restoration technicians use thermal imaging cameras and moisture meters to trace water migration and identify all affected areas immediately.

Consumer tools cannot compare with the scale of professional drying and extraction equipment. A truck-mounted unit clears water at hundreds of times the speed of a shop vac, while a commercial dehumidifier takes 10 to 20 times more moisture out of the air than a household model. Mold can begin growing in 24 to 48 hours, so this kind of power and speed matters a great deal.

Burst Pipe Repair Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Wrap any bare pipe that runs through a crawl space, garage or attic without heat
  • Never shut the furnace off completely during a winter trip, or pipes inside may freeze
  • Every few years, ask a licensed plumber to look over the system for corrosion and worn parts
  • Pressure higher than 80 PSI wears on pipes and their fittings, so test yours from time to time
  • Aging galvanized or polybutylene plumbing should be swapped out ahead of an unexpected failure
  • Find the main shutoff valve now and practice closing it so you can do it fast

How to Tell You Need Burst Pipe Repair

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Water charges that jump for no clear reason, a sign of a concealed leak
  • Damp, moldy odors around sinks, tubs or walls that hide pipes
  • Pipes coated in ice or frost when temperatures drop
  • Ceilings or walls that show damp patches, stains or a swollen bulge
  • Hearing water move through the house while nothing is turned on
  • Water pressure that falls off abruptly at every fixture
  • Rusty or discolored water coming from your taps
