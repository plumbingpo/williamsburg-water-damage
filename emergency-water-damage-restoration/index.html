Emergency Water Damage Restoration Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Going away longer than a few days? Close the main water valve first.
  • In basements and other low areas, keep documents and valuables up on shelves.
  • Save an emergency restoration company's number in your contacts ahead of time.
  • Sinks, dishwashers, washing machines and water heaters are all good spots for a leak sensor.
  • A sump pump should be tried out before storms arrive, ideally with a backup battery installed.
  • Being able to find the electrical panel quickly means wet areas can be de-energized without risk.

Why Hire a Professional for Emergency Water Damage Restoration?

The visible water is the easy part of an emergency. It is knowing where the rest of the water went. Baseboards, wall cavities and the space beneath flooring all take on water in minutes. Our team uses moisture meters and thermal imaging to find it, so the wet areas you cannot see are treated the same night instead of being found weeks later as a smell or a stain.

A night crew also brings equipment no household has on hand: truck-mounted extractors, commercial air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the space. The crew is trained to stay safe near contaminated water and wet wiring, and from hour one it records the loss in photos and moisture readings. Those records are useful if you file an insurance claim. From the first night to a dry house, a single crew stays on your job.

When to Call for Emergency Water Damage Restoration in Williamsburg

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • You have no way to locate or get to the shutoff while water continues to pour.
  • A water heater tank has failed and is emptying across the floor.
  • What is on the floor came up from the sewer, or it is discolored and smells foul.
  • Water from a floor above is streaming down interior walls.
  • The house was flooded when you walked in, with no telling how many hours it had been going.
  • Floor-level power strips, plugged-in appliances or the breaker panel are now wet.
  • Pooled water is creeping over the floor into neighboring rooms or onto the staircase.
