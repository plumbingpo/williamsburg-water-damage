Kitchen Water Damage Help in Williamsburg

Count the dishwasher, disposal, drain, faucet and fridge line, and the kitchen has more places for water to connect than anywhere else in the house. Nearly all of them are hidden inside a cabinet or behind an appliance. A drip from any of them travels to the rear of the cabinet base, then spreads beneath the flooring. We help Williamsburg homeowners find it, dry it, and save as much of the kitchen as possible.

The costly piece of a kitchen leak is the cabinets. Boxes made of particleboard swell quickly and do not shrink back, while plywood and solid wood often dry out well. Toe kicks come off and a few small, concealed holes are drilled so dry air can travel behind and below the cabinets, which lets cabinets and countertops stay put in many kitchens during drying.

Warning Signs

  • Soft or discolored vinyl flooring near the refrigerator or along the cabinet line
  • A sink cabinet bottom that feels soft, looks stained, or has warped
  • Laminate or wood floor that is lifting or cupping where you stand at the dishwasher
  • A damp, stale smell when you open the cabinet under the sink
  • The ceiling under the kitchen has developed a brown ring
  • Cabinet doors that stick or no longer line up with each other
What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Stop the Source

The fixture or appliance line that leaked is shut off, the refrigerator or dishwasher is pulled if necessary, and we confirm the failed connection so it gets repaired before we start drying.

2

Measure the Wet Area

The base of each cabinet, the wall in back and flooring all the way across the kitchen are checked with meters and a thermal camera. It is common for water beneath a kitchen floor to spread further than you would guess.

3

Dry Without Demolition

We remove the toe kicks and direct air behind and beneath the cabinets. An antimicrobial is applied to damp cavities while air movers and dehumidifiers work over several days.

4

Take Out Only What Failed

Cabinet sections, flooring or subfloor that cannot be saved are taken out carefully. Once readings confirm everything is dry, we put the appliances back and help you schedule whatever repairs are left.

If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

First hour

Water Goes Underneath

When a hose or fitting fails, water heads behind the cabinets and beneath the appliances. Seams and edges let it get below the flooring.

Hours 24 to 48

Cabinets Start Swelling

Particleboard cabinet bases and toe kicks soak up water and swell. In the dark, enclosed gap below the cabinets, and at the back of the dishwasher, mold can start.

Days 3 through 7

Flooring Starts to Move

Laminate swells at the edges, hardwood cups, and vinyl lifts once the adhesive under it fails. The doors and drawers on the cabinets go out of alignment.

After a week

Removal Becomes Likely

A cabinet box that has swollen is not coming back. The subfloor may be coming apart in layers, and mold behind cabinets often means they come out together with the countertop.

What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Wet Wall Behind Cabinets

The wall at the back of the sink and dishwasher holds drain and supply pipes. A leak there wets drywall and framing that the cabinets cover completely, so mold can spread across the wall unseen.

Toe Kick Space

The gap under base cabinets is closed on every side. Water collects there, stays for weeks, and grows mold that nobody sees until the cabinets are pulled.

Refrigerator Water Line

A few drops per minute can escape from the narrow tube that supplies the ice maker. Since the refrigerator is never pulled out, even that much rots the floor and the wall in back of it.

Layers of Flooring

It is common for a kitchen floor to be laid right over the previous one. Trapped water sits between the two, with no way to evaporate and no sign on the surface.
