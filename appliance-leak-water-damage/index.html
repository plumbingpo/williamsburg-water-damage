Dealing With Appliance Leak Water Damage in Williamsburg

Three appliances cause most of the leaks we see. Washer supply hoses can burst and keep spraying at full pressure until the valve is closed. Dishwashers usually leak a little at a time, at the drain hose or door seal, hidden beneath the cabinets. Behind a refrigerator, the thin tube feeding the ice maker may drip for months. Each one needs a different kind of cleanup.

For homeowners in Williamsburg, we begin by sliding the machine out to see how far the water traveled. For a kitchen, the usual places are below the flooring and under the cabinets. Laundry room water goes into the wall at the back of the machine and the neighboring rooms, and with an upstairs laundry, the ceiling underneath. Standing water is extracted, absorbed moisture is measured, and drying equipment is sized to fit the wet area.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut the Machine DownIf the outlet and the floor next to it are both dry, end the cycle and take the plug out of the wall. If not, switch it off at the breaker instead.
  2. Close the Supply ValveFor a washing machine, close both the cold and hot valves on the wall at its back. The dishwasher valve is under the kitchen sink. A refrigerator line has a small valve below or behind the unit.
  3. Soak Up Standing WaterMop or towel up what you can reach so it stops spreading under cabinets and flooring. Use a vacuum only if it is a wet and dry model.
  4. Keep the Door ClosedA dishwasher or washer that still holds water should stay closed. When the door opens mid-cycle, the full load of water can pour out on the floor.
  5. Photograph the Failed PartTake pictures of the hose, valve or fitting that failed and keep the part. Both the appliance maker and your insurer may want to look at it.
Coverage

Insurance and Appliance Leak Water Damage

When a machine overflows unexpectedly or a hose bursts, that is typically treated as sudden and accidental, which many homeowners policies cover. What is typically paid is the water damage, while the failed part and the appliance are left out. A claim is harder when an ice maker line or dishwasher seeped slowly, since coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks. How old the hose was and how it was maintained may also come up with some insurers. Hold on to the part that failed, photograph everything ahead of cleanup, and review what your policy says.

The Usual Culprits

What Causes It

  • The water inlet valve on a washer or dishwasher failing to close, which lets the machine overfill.
  • Installation mistakes like a loose supply line connection or a dishwasher that does not sit level.
  • Washer supply hoses made of rubber that get weak over the years and burst from the pressure.
  • Water under a refrigerator from a blocked defrost drain or a drain pan with a crack in it.
  • A washer drain hose that slips out of the standpipe, or a clogged standpipe.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Wall Bases and Trim

On a flooded kitchen or laundry floor, the baseboards and the lower edge of the drywall draw water upward. Often the worst of it is on the wall in back of the appliance, where it went unseen.

Floors and What Is Under Them

A leak runs under laminate, hardwood or vinyl plank flooring with little resistance. Boards cup or swell, and the subfloor underneath stays wet long after the surface looks dry.

Downstairs Ceiling Damage

Water from a second floor laundry room drops through the floor and collects on the ceiling below. Drywall there sags and stains while the insulation over it holds water.

Cabinet Damage

Dishwasher leaks soak cabinet sides and toe kicks from underneath. The panels swell and separate, and wet cabinet boxes can hold moisture against the wall for weeks.
