Structural drying is the most important phase of water damage restoration, and the one untrained responders most often get wrong. Extraction takes care of standing water, but drywall, framing, subflooring, insulation, concrete and other building materials stay loaded with trapped moisture. A structure left wet ends up with odors, wood rot, mold and failing materials, so the moisture has to be removed in a scientific way using dehumidification and controlled evaporation.

Williamsburg Restoration employs IICRC-certified structural drying technicians who understand psychrometry, the study of temperature, humidity and moisture relationships in air. That knowledge lets them work out where equipment goes, what temperature to hold and how air should move so evaporation is as effective as possible on every job. Plaster, concrete, hardwood and drywall each dry at their own rate, so each needs its own approach to get down to an acceptable moisture level with no added damage.

Our structural drying process includes daily moisture monitoring with calibrated moisture meters, thermo-hygrometers and thermal imaging cameras. Readings are recorded from start to finish in a detailed drying log, which shows moisture dropping step by step in every affected material. Equipment is adjusted daily based on these readings to optimize drying efficiency. Drying is complete only when all affected materials reach acceptable moisture standards as defined by IICRC S500 guidelines, not when surfaces simply feel dry to the touch.

Warning Signs for Structural Drying

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Humidity that will not come down in the affected space, whatever ventilation you try
  • Window glass that fogs up near the affected rooms
  • Swollen trim and baseboards separating at the seams
  • Wallboard with water stains, puffiness or a spongy feel
  • Wood flooring with boards that cup, crown or pull apart
  • Blistered paint and lifting wallpaper on a wall that got wet
  • Materials breaking down in a spot that got wet before and was not dried by professionals

What a Professional Brings to Structural Drying

Structural drying is a science, not a guessing game. Secondary mold damage most often traces back to someone who put down fans and hoped things would dry. It involves daily monitoring, a calculation of the required grain depression (the capacity to remove moisture) and a working knowledge of the way materials absorb moisture and let it go. Lacking that understanding, people either stop too soon and leave moisture for mold, or dry too hard and harm wood and other materials.

Consumer products and the equipment professionals dry structures with have very little in common. Commercial LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers remove 10 to 20 times more moisture from the air than residential dehumidifiers, and high-velocity air movers create the precise airflow patterns needed to accelerate evaporation from structural surfaces. Between the investment in equipment and the training needed to run it, it is no surprise that DIY drying falls well short of a professional job.

How Our Structural Drying Works

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's how we handle structural drying from start to finish:

1

Moisture Assessment

We build a complete moisture map of every affected area with hygrometers, thermal imaging and moisture meters. From that map we decide where equipment goes and record our baseline readings.

2

Equipment Deployment

Guided by the moisture map, we place high-velocity air movers and commercial LGR dehumidifiers where they will do the most good. Temperature and airflow are optimized for the specific materials being dried.

3

Daily Checks and Adjustments

Our technician returns daily to take moisture readings at all monitoring points, adjust equipment placement and document drying progress. We keep you posted on how the timeline looks.

4

Dry Standard Verification

Final readings are documented, the equipment comes out and your property is certified at dry standard as soon as all affected materials test at acceptable levels.

Structural Drying Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Plan for daily access so readings can be taken and equipment adjusted
  • Avoid moving the machines yourself, because their placement follows our moisture map
  • Let air movers and dehumidifiers run continuously, 24/7, until the technician removes them
  • Shut the doors and windows of any room under drying, which keeps dehumidification working well
  • Trust the moisture meter readings, because surfaces can feel dry while moisture remains deep in materials
  • Do not place personal items on top of drying equipment or block the airflow from air movers
