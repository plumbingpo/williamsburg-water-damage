Hardwood Floor Water Damage in Williamsburg

It does not take long for water to change a wood floor. Boards take on moisture from below and from the edges, swell against each other and start to cup, with the edges rising higher than the center. Cupping alone is no reason to give up on the floor. Boards of solid wood often return to flat if they are dried during those early days. The key is pulling moisture out of the boards and the subfloor underneath at a steady, controlled pace.

We dry hardwood with floor drying mats. The mats are sealed to the surface and connected to a vacuum unit that draws moisture up through the boards and seams, while dehumidifiers keep the room air dry. Compared with carpet or drywall it is slower, commonly a week or more. Sanding has to wait until the wood is fully dry. If cupped boards are sanded while still wet, crowning shows up down the road.

What It Looks Like

  • Cupping, where each board's edges rise above its middle
  • Fasteners working loose, heard as squeaks where the floor was soaked
  • Blackish or gray discoloration at board joints and fasteners
  • Mildew smell coming up through the boards
  • Puffy baseboards and casings next to the affected boards
  • Gaps opening between boards after a floor has dried too fast
Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Damage Beyond the Room

Water under a floor travels. It can reach under cabinets, wall plates and into neighboring rooms, or drip through to the ceiling of the level beneath.

Refinishing Before It Is Dry

Sanding cupped boards flat while they are still wet removes the raised edges. When the wood finishes drying, the centers end up high and the floor is crowned.

Drying Too Fast

Heavy airflow or heat dries the face of a board well ahead of its core. The result can be cracks, splits and permanent gaps between boards.

Moisture Under the Boards

The subfloor can hold moisture long after the boards feel dry. Unless that layer is dried as well, expect renewed cupping or mold on the underside.

What We Do

Our Hardwood Floor Water Damage Process

1

Take Baseline Readings

Readings are taken in the boards and the subfloor, and on dry flooring close by as a reference. That tells us how deep the water went and whether saving the floor is realistic.

2

Remove Surface Water

Rugs and furnishings come off the affected boards while pooled water is vacuumed up at once. Cutting off the water entering the joints early keeps swelling down.

3

Set Drying Mats

We tape drying mats to the floor and attach them to a vacuum system, which draws moisture upward through the wood. The room is managed with dehumidifiers so drying stays even and the boards do not crack.

4

Track Progress and Report

Every day or two we take new readings, continuing until the subfloor and boards match the dry reference spots. Then we tell you whether the floor needs refinishing, a few boards replaced, or nothing.

If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

First hours

Water Goes Under

Most of the water ends up beneath the boards, having run down through the joints. You can towel off the top and still have a saturated underside.

24 to 48 hours

Cupping Starts

The edges come up as each board expands. Finish may turn cloudy. Growth is able to begin below, on raw wood and on the subfloor panels.

Days 3 through 7

Pressure Builds Up

Expanding boards push harder and harder on one another. With fasteners losing their hold and boards compressing at the edges, some areas can lift away from the subfloor.

Past one week

Permanent Damage

Expect a good chance of mold below, dark staining and boards that have buckled. By now replacement, not drying, is the answer for most of the affected section.
