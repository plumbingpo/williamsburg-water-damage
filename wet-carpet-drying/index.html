Wet Carpet Drying: What Williamsburg Homeowners Should Know

Carpet holds far more water than it looks like it does. The face fibers can feel only damp while the pad underneath is soaked like a sponge and the subfloor below it is wet too. A shop vacuum and a stack of towels only deal with the surface. In Williamsburg, we pull the water out, take moisture meter readings on each layer, and place drying equipment that works down to the subfloor.

Saving a carpet comes down to how long it stayed wet and whether the water that soaked it was clean. When a clean supply line is the source and we arrive early, drying the carpet where it lies usually works. With an appliance leak, which is gray water, we often discard the pad, then clean and treat the carpet. Both layers, carpet and pad, are removed when the water was sewage or came in from outside flooding.

Signs to Look For

  • Socks that come away damp, or a squishing sound, when you cross the carpet
  • Under the wet section, the floor gives a little or squeaks when you step on it
  • Carpet that has come off the tack strip, leaving loose areas or ripples
  • Dark, rusty lines along the wall where the tack strip has gotten wet
  • A clammy, cool patch underfoot that lingers well past the original spill
  • Paint blistering near the floor, or baseboard trim that has puffed up
The Clock

The Damage Timeline

First hours

Water Moves Outward

Water travels through the pad far beyond the patch you can see. The tack strip starts taking on water as it creeps below the walls toward the room next door.

Hours 24 to 48

Smell and Mold Start

The damp pad breeds bacteria, and the sour smell of wet carpet follows. Mold can start to grow on dust caught deep in the pile and on the carpet back.

Day 3 to 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

As the latex bonding the backing gives out, the carpet delaminates and starts to ripple. Rust forms on the tack strip nails while the subfloor underneath swells.

Past one week

Past Saving

By now the carpet and pad usually have to come out. The work gets bigger, since mold is often on the subfloor and the lower edge of the drywall.

Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

Swollen Subfloor

Left wet, the OSB or particleboard beneath a carpet swells and then crumbles. The soft spots, squeaking and lifted seams appear weeks afterward, when nobody remembers the spill.

Water Held in the Pad

Water many times heavier than the pad itself can sit inside it. It keeps feeding moisture into the carpet above and the floor below long after the surface feels dry.

Clean Water Turns Dirty

Even clean water goes bad the longer it sits. It collects bacteria and dirt from the pad and fibers, which can turn a simple drying job into a removal.

Stains From Furniture

Wood stain and metal feet on furniture bleed into wet carpet. Those marks are often permanent, and the furniture itself wicks water and swells.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Find the Edges

Our first job is finding the outer limit of the water, under walls and in closets too, using a thermal camera and moisture meters. The water category gets identified as well, since it determines what we can keep.

2

Extract the Water

Weighted extraction tools press water out of the carpet and the pad together. This removes far more than a wand alone, and it shortens the drying time that follows.

3

Dry All the Layers

The carpet is either pulled back so the pad can go, or left down with air blown beneath it, whichever the job needs. Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on until the subfloor and the carpet each test dry.

4

Clean and Reset

Where needed, an antimicrobial goes on the subfloor and carpet, then new pad is laid if the old was removed, and the carpet is stretched back on its tack strip and cleaned.
