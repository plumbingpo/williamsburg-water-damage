Crawl Space Water Removal: What Williamsburg Homeowners Should Know

A flooded crawl space rarely announces itself. It tends to turn up as a damp chill underfoot, a stale smell at the vents, or a small lake discovered the day somebody lifts the hatch. At that point the batts are typically saturated and the framing overhead has sat damp for several days. Crews from our company extract the water, haul away anything that cannot be saved and dry the structure ahead of rot.

Once the standing water is gone, the job is only halfway done. Wood framing takes water in gradually and gives it up just as gradually, which is why we meter the joists, beams and subfloor and leave dehumidifiers on until readings drop, typically 3 to 5 days. We also look for where the water came from, a leaking supply line, a drain, or groundwater after rain, so it does not fill back up.

Signs to Look For

  • Tide lines or white mineral crust on the piers and foundation walls
  • Flooring overhead that seems chilly, clammy, spongy or springy underfoot
  • Corroded metal such as duct straps, pipe hangers and steel posts
  • Joists, beams or subfloor panels coated with whitish or blackish fuzz
  • Water beading and dripping off cold pipes or metal ducts under the floor
  • Fiberglass insulation sagging or hanging down between the floor joists
Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Settling Under the House

Saturated soil can soften around footings and let piers settle or lean. Sloping floors or sticking doors in the rooms above may point to this.

Pests Drawn to Moisture

Damp wood and wet soil are exactly what termites, carpenter ants and rodents look for. Drying the space removes much of what attracts them.

Electrical and Duct Hazards

Under a typical house you will find cable runs, electrical boxes and duct lines. When duct insulation is saturated it sends stale air around the home, while flooded wiring creates a real risk of shock.

What You Breathe Upstairs

Because air naturally moves upward from under the home, spores and stale odors in a wet crawl space reach your living areas too.

What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Safety Check and Inspection

We check for electrical hazards, sagging ducts and contaminated water before anyone crawls in. Next comes charting the extent of the water and metering the joists and subfloor for moisture.

2

Pump Out Water

Submersible pumps and extraction hoses remove the standing water, including the low spots and footing trenches where it collects. Any silt or mud sitting on the liner gets removed during this step.

3

Clear Out Ruined Material

Debris, saturated batts and any liner that cannot be reused leave the crawl space in bags. Our crew then wipes down the uncovered framing and applies antimicrobial to spots with growth or a high chance of it.

4

Final Drying and Checks

Compact fans and dehumidification work in the crawl space as long as it takes for the framing to test normal. You get the closing moisture readings plus a plan for keeping water out, after we verify everything by meter.

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

First day

Puddles Form Underneath

The dirt floor starts absorbing moisture as puddles form in every dip. Insulation starts wicking it up and humidity under the floor climbs fast.

24 to 48 hours

Mold Gets Started

Mold can start on damp joists and paper faced insulation. Soaked insulation gains weight and starts to sag off the floor above.

Day 3 to 7

Moisture Moves Upstairs

Humid air from below works its way up into the rooms. Floors may start to cup, rooms smell musty and the wood framing keeps absorbing water.

After a week

Wood Starts Rotting

Decay begins in any framing that never got a chance to dry. Expect softer framing, rusted fasteners, and a wet environment that draws in termites and similar pests.
