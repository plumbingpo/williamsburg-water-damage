Bathroom Water Damage: What Williamsburg Homeowners Should Know

A bathroom is designed to handle water on its surfaces, never below them. Once caulk splits, grout thins out or the toilet seal gives up, each shower and flush sends a bit of water through. That water gathers where you cannot see it, in the subfloor and inside the tiled wall. Our Williamsburg team finds where the water is getting in, measures how far it has gone, and dries or removes what is wet.

Slow leaks are the norm here, which means what you find has typically been there longer, and gone further, than it seems. Subfloor under a toilet can be soft all the way through while the tile above still looks fine. With thermal imaging and moisture meters, we map what is wet before any demolition, and we open just the sections that will not dry in place.

What It Looks Like

  • A spongy feel in the floor beside the tub or in front of the vanity
  • Grout lines that stay dark and damp long after the last shower
  • Inside the vanity, a base shelf that is stained, soft or out of shape
  • Edges of the vinyl floor peeling up, or dark discoloration showing through it
  • Dark stains at the base of the toilet, or a bowl that wobbles a little
  • Directly under the bathroom, a ceiling with a brown stain or a sag
How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Find the Leak

We test the tub, shower, toilet and supply lines one at a time, and use moisture meters and thermal imaging to see where water has traveled under tile and inside walls.

2

Open What Is Needed

Loose tile, rotted subfloor and soft wall board come out. A toilet leak means contamination, so anything porous that will not come clean is removed.

3

Dry the Hidden Spaces

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the subfloor, the wall framing and the ceiling space below. Nothing is sealed back up until we have treated with antimicrobial and the readings show dry.

4

Rebuild and Reseal

We replace subfloor and backer board, and can coordinate tile, a new toilet seal, fresh caulk and ceiling repair so the leak path is closed, not covered over.

If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

First days

Water Slips Through

A little water passes the failed grout or caulk every time someone showers. Nothing shows yet, but the subfloor edge and the board behind the tile are absorbing it.

The first several weeks

Subfloor Stays Wet

Showers come too often for the wood beneath the tile to ever dry. Within the wall cavity, and across the bottom of the floor, mold begins.

Months one to three

Signs Show Up

Grout darkens, tiles loosen, and the caulk line turns black. If there is a room below, a stain appears on its ceiling and slowly widens.

Six months or more

The Structure Suffers

Around the tub or toilet the subfloor goes soft, and the joists may start rotting. The repair now includes carpentry and new tile, not only drying.

What You Cannot See

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Mold Behind Tile

By themselves, grout and tile do not keep water out. When the layer behind them stays wet, mold grows on the wall board where cleaning never reaches.

Leaks Inside the Vanity

Behind the towels and bottles kept under the sink, a drain fitting or supply valve can drip for months. First the bottom of the cabinet swells, and then the wall behind and the subfloor get wet.

Poor Ventilation

A weak or missing exhaust fan leaves steam in the room after every shower. It condenses on the ceiling and walls, encourages mold, and keeps already wet materials from drying.

Damage Downstairs

Before dripping, water runs along joists and pipes, which can put the downstairs stain several feet away from the real leak. Its path can cross electrical wiring and ceiling lights.
