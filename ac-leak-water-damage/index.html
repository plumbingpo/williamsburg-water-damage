AC Leak Water Damage Help in Williamsburg

Your air conditioner pulls moisture out of the air all day, and that water has to go somewhere. A pan under the coil catches the drips, and from there a thin pipe empties the pan. Algae and dust can plug the line, and then the pan fills up and overflows. If the air handler sits in a hallway closet or the attic, that water ends up in the nearby floor, wall or ceiling.

A slow drip causes more harm than most homeowners would guess. It soaks the stand the unit sits on, climbs into the drywall and spreads beneath the flooring, and by the time you see a mark mold has frequently begun. Tracking the full extent of the moisture, drying it and making repairs is our part. Clearing the drain or repairing the unit remains a job for an HVAC technician, and our schedule can fit around theirs.

Do This First

Before We Arrive

  1. Shut the System DownNo more condensate forms once the AC is turned off at the thermostat. Flip the air handler's breaker too when water is close to its wiring.
  2. Avoid Wet Wiring and LightsDo not touch the unit, nearby outlets or ceiling lights that have water in or around them until power to that area is off.
  3. Clear Water From the PanUse a wet-vac or rags to take the standing water out of the pan and stop the spill, provided the pan is safe to reach. Skip this step if it means crossing a soaked attic.
  4. Soak Up What You CanBlot wet carpet, wipe hard floors, set a bucket under any ceiling drip, and move boxes, clothes and furniture away from the closet or the stained area.
  5. Call Both TradesYou need two calls: one to us so we can measure the wet area, one to an HVAC technician who will unclog the drain. Until that repair is done, the unit stays off.
Coverage

What About Insurance?

With AC leaks, insurers mostly look at how long the water was running. If the pan overflowed suddenly and you acted when you found it, many homeowners policies pay for the resulting water damage. A drip the adjuster believes lasted weeks or months can be classed as gradual leakage or poor maintenance, and the claim denied. Repairing the air conditioner itself is typically not covered either way. Check your policy, and we can supply readings and photos for the claim.

Why It Happens

What Causes It

  • The drain pipe for condensate blocked by a buildup of dust and algae.
  • A rusted, cracked or out of level drain pan that lets water escape.
  • A dirty filter or low refrigerant, both of which make the coil ice up.
  • More meltwater than the pan can handle after an iced-over coil thaws.
  • The safety float switch being absent or broken, so the unit kept running.
What Is at Risk

What the Water Damages

Drywall Below the Air Handler

When the air handler is overhead, the ceiling under it takes the water. The ceiling stains first, then sags, and a section soaked for days can fall into the hallway or bedroom below.

Mold Near the Return

Within 24 to 48 hours mold can start on wet wood and drywall next to the unit, and the return may draw spores into the ducts and around the home.

Floors Outside the Closet

From the closet, water slips under the door and works below hardwood, laminate or carpet pad. Swollen laminate does not go back, and wet hardwood left alone will cup.

The Platform and Closet Walls

Many closet installations put the air handler on a wood platform, with the return box below it. The wood rots and the nearby drywall draws moisture upward, all of it out of sight in back of the unit.

What We Do

Our AC Leak Water Damage Process

1

Find Every Wet Area

With thermal imaging and moisture meters, our team checks the unit, the adjacent walls or ceiling and the floor beyond the closet, so wet materials are found even where no stain shows.

2

Extract and Open Up

Standing water in the pan, the platform and the carpet is extracted. To let the space behind it breathe, we cut away drywall and insulation that are soaked or growing mold.

3

Set Drying Equipment

Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on for a few days, usually 3 to 5, and we log the moisture readings. Access to the air handler is kept open for your HVAC technician.

4

Clean and Restore

Affected surfaces get an antimicrobial treatment, because condensate that has sat for days is rarely clean. After that, our crew puts back the drywall, insulation, baseboard and floor and paints the repair to blend in.
